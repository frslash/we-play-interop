# A public side for InWove (a suggestion, not built)

*Status: a suggestion for Project Weave and the room's people to decide on. Nothing here is built, and nothing
here changes the members' room.*

## Why

InWove's members' room is where people building the trust layer work together with their agents. It is being given
to Project Weave as a community people join with a wallet (Keyring). That suits people who already build with these
tools. Most people who could use this technology never get that far, because they would have to install a wallet
before they could even look around.

Project Weave describes its work as "accelerating the interoperability of the internet's missing trust layer"
(https://projectweave.tech): helping programmers implement shared protocols that plug into each other, for
movements such as civic technology, Indigenous data sovereignty and bioregional regeneration
(https://projectweave.tech/funders.html). A public side of InWove would be an open front door to that work: a place
where anyone, with no wallet, can learn what the shared protocols are, find out who already builds what, and get
help building something that works with them.

## What it could hold

1. **The stack in plain words.** What each shared piece is for (decentralized trust graph credentials, Trust Tasks,
   the trust spanning protocol, groups), written for someone who has never heard of any of them. Drawn from
   `docs/interop-facts.md`, and kept cited so it stays checkable.
2. **Who builds what.** The DTG interop board's data (`Project-Weave-Tech/dtg-interop`) shown as a map: which
   projects exist, what each one implements, and which pairs have been shown to work together.
3. **"I want to build…" paths.** Pick a goal (a membership card for a group, signing in with a wallet, an encrypted
   shared space, an agent that acts for a person) and get the protocols involved, the projects that already do it,
   a starting point, and the tests that show it works with the others.
4. **The method.** The integration protocol (`Project-Weave-Tech/inwove-integration-protocol`): how to make one
   project work with another project's real software, by hand or with AI agents.
5. **A helper agent that reads only public material.** It answers questions from the facts, the board and the
   guides, cites where each answer came from, and has no access to the members' room.
6. **A way to reach the members.** A question or proposal from the public side arrives in the members' room as one
   item, and a member answers in public if they choose to. Nothing from the members' room appears on the public
   side unless a member puts it there.
7. **A path into Project Weave's programmes**, such as its fellowship and its paid protocol implementation contracts,
   for builders who are ready.

## Who it is for

Builders and community organisers who know what they want to make for their community, and may use an AI assistant
to make it, but are not identity or cryptography specialists.

## How it relates to the members' room

- It is separate. Reading needs no sign-in. Posting a question, if that is allowed, needs at most a passkey. No
  wallet, ever.
- The members' room stays members-only and encrypted. The public side holds only what is public on purpose.
- The same facts page and the same board feed both, so they never disagree.

## What would show it working, and failing

- **Working:** someone with no wallet and no background finds, in one visit, the protocols and an existing project
  for what they want to build. Projects started from it appear on the interop board with a passing run. Questions
  asked there get answered, and the answers flow back into the plain-words guide.
- **Failing:** people leave at the first page of jargon; the helper agent says things the facts page does not
  support; the members' room fills with public questions nobody answers.
- **Where to look:** counts only (visits that reach a path's last step, questions asked and answered, board entries
  that name the public side as where they started), never who visited.

## Open questions for Project Weave

1. Project Weave's site says it does not build technology or run platforms. Does a public side fit that, and who
   would keep it: Project Weave, the room's people, or a project on the board?
2. Should public questions reach the members' room at all, and who answers them?
3. Which programmes should it point to, and how does a builder know they are ready for one?
